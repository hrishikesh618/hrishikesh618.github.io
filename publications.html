Publications

Published journal articles and the book chapter, first-author and co-authored alike (each card is tagged), sortable by date, journal impact factor, or citation count. Manuscripts still under review or in preparation are listed separately at the bottom, since they don't yet have citation or impact-factor data.

  1. 2025

    Water Resources Management

    First-author Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. (2025). Performance of ERA5 products in capturing pluvial and fluvial inundation: a statistical-cum-hydrodynamic framework for resource-constrained large flood-prone watersheds. Water Resources Management.

    Impact factor 5.13 (2024) 6 citations (Google Scholar, Jul 2026)

    DOI

    Rigorously evaluates ERA5 rainfall and GloFAS discharge against station observations, then simulates past flood events (2006, 2008, 2011) in LISFLOOD-FP with hit rates exceeding 0.8 against MODIS imagery, producing 50-, 100- and 200-year design flood-hazard maps expressed as depth and flood momentum.

  2. 2025

    IJAEOG

    First-author Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. (2025). Multi-scale assessment and entropy-MCDM framework for evaluating reanalysis precipitation datasets over Indian basins. International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation, 144, 104919.

    Impact factor 9.83 (2024) 4 citations (Google Scholar, Jul 2026)

    DOI

    Systematically evaluates ERA5, IMDAA, MERRA-2 and CFSR/CFSv2 against IMD observations across 25 Indian basins (1990–2024) using 11 statistical metrics, then ranks datasets with a Shannon Entropy-TOPSIS decision framework. ERA5 emerges as the most consistent dataset nationally; IMDAA performs best in monsoon and mountainous basins.

Under review / in preparation

These don't yet have citation or impact-factor data, so they sit outside the sortable timeline above.

Under internal review Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. A nationwide ranking of India's 100 Smart Cities under disparate urban flood risk.

Status: under internal review. No target journal is named publicly at this stage.

Introduces the Tier-based Risk Index for Cities (TRIC), a copula-based framework quantifying compound pluvial-fluvial flood risk across India's 100 Smart Cities. Finds 10.35 million people (7.64%) and US$147.48 billion in GDP (9.03%) exposed to High/Very-High hazard; identifies Surat, Ahmedabad, Chinchwad, Visakhapatnam, Kalyan and Guwahati as Tier-5 (highest-risk) cities.
