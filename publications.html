Publications

Published first-author journal articles (thesis foundation)

Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. (2025). Performance of ERA5 products in capturing pluvial and fluvial inundation: a statistical-cum-hydrodynamic framework for resource-constrained large flood-prone watersheds. Water Resources Management.

DOI

Rigorously evaluates ERA5 rainfall and GloFAS discharge against station observations, then simulates past flood events (2006, 2008, 2011) in LISFLOOD-FP with hit rates exceeding 0.8 against MODIS imagery, producing 50-, 100- and 200-year design flood-hazard maps expressed as depth and flood momentum.

Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. (2025). Multi-scale assessment and entropy-MCDM framework for evaluating reanalysis precipitation datasets over Indian basins. International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation, 144, 104919.

DOI

Systematically evaluates ERA5, IMDAA, MERRA-2 and CFSR/CFSv2 against IMD observations across 25 Indian basins (1990–2024) using 11 statistical metrics, then ranks datasets with a Shannon Entropy-TOPSIS decision framework. ERA5 emerges as the most consistent dataset nationally; IMDAA performs best in monsoon and mountainous basins.

Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. (2023). Can atmospheric reanalysis datasets reproduce flood inundation at regional scales? A systematic analysis with ERA5 over Mahanadi River Basin, India. Environmental Monitoring and Assessment, 195, 1143.

DOI

The foundational study of the thesis: tests whether ERA5 can reproduce historical flood inundation (2006, 2008, 2011, 2014) over the Mahanadi River Basin using LISFLOOD-FP, validated against MODIS satellite imagery with hit rates exceeding 0.8.

First-author manuscripts under review / in preparation

Under review Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. A nationwide ranking of India's 100 Smart Cities under disparate urban flood risk.

Status: journal status to be confirmed by Hrishikesh (source documents disagree on whether this is under review at Nature Cities or Nature Communications Earth and Environment).

Introduces the Tier-based Risk Index for Cities (TRIC), a copula-based framework quantifying compound pluvial-fluvial flood risk across India's 100 Smart Cities. Finds 10.35 million people (7.64%) and US$147.48 billion in GDP (9.03%) exposed to High/Very-High hazard; identifies Surat, Ahmedabad, Chinchwad, Visakhapatnam, Kalyan and Guwahati as Tier-5 (highest-risk) cities.

In preparation Singh, H. and Mohanty, M.P. Multiday storm event reveals differential flood resilience across Indian basins.

Manuscript in advanced preparation; target: Geophysical Research Letters.
