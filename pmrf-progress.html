PMRF Progress

PMRF Yearly Progress Report: May 2026 · PMRF Research Scholar, Fellowship Cycle 11

Ph.D. Supervisor: Prof. Mohit Prakash Mohanty, Department of Water Resources Development and Management, IIT Roorkee

Research challenge

India is the second most flood-affected nation in the world, yet still lacks a consolidated, high-resolution national database of flood inundation, hazard and risk. Large parts of India's flood-prone domain, particularly the Himalayan, Northern, Eastern and North-eastern states, remain ungauged or sparsely gauged, making conventional numerical flood modelling untenable at the national scale. This doctoral research addresses that gap by developing an integrated, high-resolution flood-risk framework for India that leverages reanalysis-based hydro-meteorological inputs and physics-based hydrodynamic modelling to quantify risk at fine administrative scale, jointly capturing hazard, socio-economic-cum-physical vulnerability, and exposure.

Thesis overview

Flood risk is quantified jointly at the basin scale and the city scale. At the basin scale, risk is the interaction between a nationwide depth-based hydrodynamic flood hazard (LISFLOOD-FP, forced by multi-day design rainfall and discharge) and a district-level socio-economic-cum-physical vulnerability index built from 54 indicators using a Shannon Entropy-TOPSIS scheme. At the city scale, risk is the interaction between a momentum-based urban flood hazard (from city-specific copula-conditioned design forcings) and population-cum-GDP exposure at fine grid resolution.

TRIC: the Tier-based Risk Index for Cities

TRIC derives city-specific compound flood forcings rather than assuming uniform national design events. Bivariate Archimedean copulas are used for pluvial-only cities (using Rx1day and N25), while a trivariate Gumbel-Hougaard copula is used for 51 river-influenced cities by additionally coupling annual-maximum discharge. The resulting joint-severity design events are translated into design hydrographs and design rainfall hyetographs, then simulated with a momentum-based hazard rating (Haz = h(Vc + 1.5)) using the GPU build of LISFLOOD-FP, integrated with WorldPop population and downscaled GDP-PPP exposure.

Across the 100-city network, 10.35 million people (7.64%) and US$147.48 billion in GDP (9.03%) fall within High or Very-High hazard zones. Six Tier-5 cities — Surat, Ahmedabad, Chinchwad, Visakhapatnam, Kalyan and Guwahati — and 27 Tier-4 cities form the national priority set.
